**Afghan Hub Multilingual Community Navigation Pilot**

**Urban Community Grant — Community Resilience Building**

Secondary fit to test against the live form:
**Intercultural Understanding and Exchange**

## Short project summary

Afghan Hub proposes a multilingual community navigation pilot to make it easier for Afghan British Columbians to discover community organizations, events, volunteer opportunities, professional connections and other participation opportunities.

Community information is often spread across websites, social media pages and informal networks. People may know what they need but not the organization name, category or institutional terminology required to find it. These barriers can be greater for newcomers, people with smaller local networks, or people who prefer Dari or Pashto.

The pilot will test a simple read-only digital navigation experience in English, Dari and Pashto. Community participants will use guided natural-language prompts to find relevant public community information, then provide structured feedback on clarity, accessibility and usefulness.

The project will focus on inclusion, participation and community connection. It will not provide automated legal or immigration advice and will not make autonomous decisions or applications on behalf of users.

## What problem will the project address?

The project addresses three connected barriers:

1. **Fragmented community information.** Events, organizations and opportunities are distributed across many websites and informal channels.
2. **Language and terminology barriers.** Community members may know the outcome they need without knowing the formal category or wording used by a directory or organization.
3. **Unequal access to networks.** People with smaller local social or professional networks may be less likely to hear about opportunities through word of mouth.

These barriers can reduce participation and make it harder for community members to connect with organizations and opportunities that already exist.

## Project goal

To test whether a multilingual, community-informed digital navigation tool can improve access to community information and strengthen participation, connection and awareness among Afghan British Columbians.

## Objectives

1. Improve access to community organizations, activities and participation opportunities.
2. Test English, Dari and Pashto discovery language with real community participants.
3. Identify common terminology and navigation barriers.
4. Strengthen connections between community members and participating organizations.
5. Produce a short evidence-based community-learning report to guide future inclusive digital services.

## Activities

### Activity 1 — Multilingual discovery design

Prepare and review English, Dari and Pashto prompts for common community goals, including:
- finding local organizations;
- discovering community events;
- finding volunteer opportunities;
- finding professional/community connections;
- finding education, mentorship or employment-related opportunities.

### Activity 2 — Community partner review

Invite community organizations to review:
- terminology;
- public-resource discoverability;
- trust and safety cues;
- areas where users commonly struggle to find information.

### Activity 3 — Structured community testing

Run at least two structured usability/feedback sessions with community participants.

Testing will examine:
- whether users can find a relevant destination;
- which words or categories are confusing;
- whether multilingual prompts improve usability;
- which resources users expect to see;
- what the system should never automate.

### Activity 4 — Limited pilot

Make the read-only navigation experience available to a limited group of Afghan Hub users.

The pilot will direct users to underlying Afghan Hub records and public community information. It will not autonomously message organizations, submit applications or make legal/immigration decisions.

### Activity 5 — Evaluation and community-learning report

Measure pilot outcomes and prepare a concise report summarizing:
- successful resource discovery;
- zero-result patterns;
- result click-through;
- language usage;
- participant feedback;
- recommended accessibility and terminology improvements.

## Who will participate?

Primary participants:
- Afghan community members living in British Columbia;
- both newcomers and established community members;
- English, Dari and Pashto users;
- professionals, students, volunteers and community participants.

Community organizations will also be invited to provide feedback on terminology and resource discoverability.

The final application should include a realistic participant estimate once the applicant organization and recruitment partners are confirmed.

## How will diverse participants be engaged?

The project will use deliberate outreach through Afghan community organizations and networks.

Participation will be encouraged across:
- different age groups;
- different levels of digital confidence;
- English, Dari and Pashto language preferences;
- newcomers and longer-term residents;
- people seeking community, volunteer, professional or education-related opportunities.

Community partners will be invited to help identify gaps in participation and suggest additional outreach where appropriate.

## Expected outputs

- multilingual English/Dari/Pashto navigation prompts;
- at least two structured community feedback sessions;
- community partner terminology/resource review;
- limited read-only navigation pilot;
- pilot usage and accessibility findings;
- final community-learning report.

## Expected outcomes

### Short-term outcomes
- participants find relevant community information more easily;
- participants become more aware of organizations, events and opportunities;
- improved understanding of multilingual terminology and navigation barriers.

### Medium-term outcomes
- stronger participation in community activities;
- improved connections between community members and organizations;
- better evidence for designing inclusive digital community infrastructure.

## How will success be measured?

The project will use privacy-minimal indicators such as:

- percentage of searches producing at least one result;
- result click-through rate;
- zero-result rate;
- destination mix across organizations/events/opportunities/people;
- interface language usage;
- participant-reported ease of use;
- qualitative feedback from community partners;
- number of tested terminology/accessibility improvements.

Raw user search text and personal profile information will not be required for grant evaluation metrics.

## Community benefit

The project is intended to make existing community resources easier to discover rather than create a separate service silo.

By helping people find organizations, activities and opportunities through language that feels natural to them, the pilot can support participation, belonging and stronger connections across the Afghan community and with the broader B.C. community.

## Why technology is appropriate

The technology is only the delivery mechanism.

The project objective is not to fund a generic AI product. The community benefit comes from:
- multilingual access;
- clearer navigation;
- community-informed terminology;
- improved discoverability of existing public resources;
- measurable participation outcomes.

The pilot is deliberately read-only and limited so that accessibility and community usefulness can be tested before any broader automation is considered.

## Responsible technology safeguards

- read-only discovery during the pilot;
- no automated legal/immigration eligibility decisions;
- no autonomous applications or messages;
- no sale of user data;
- existing authenticated access controls;
- minimum-data approach;
- no raw search text required for pilot analytics;
- links to underlying records/resources;
- multilingual community testing before expansion.
