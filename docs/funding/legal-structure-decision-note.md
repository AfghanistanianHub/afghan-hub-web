# Afghan Hub — Funding Legal-Structure Decision Note

## Why this decision matters

Afghan Hub now has credible funding paths in two different categories:

1. community/public-benefit funding; and
2. commercial/innovation funding.

These programs often require different applicant types and should not be mixed casually.

## Track A — Community / public benefit

Examples:
- B.C. Inclusive Communities Grants
- future settlement/inclusion funding
- community foundations
- nonprofit partnerships

Typical applicant requirements:
- registered nonprofit / charity / eligible community organization;
- community-serving mandate;
- project outcomes tied to inclusion, participation, access, education or settlement.

Best Afghan Hub components:
- multilingual community navigation;
- community testing;
- inclusion/accessibility work;
- volunteer/event/resource discovery;
- community partner engagement;
- pilot evaluation.

## Track B — Commercial / innovation

Examples:
- Innovate BC Commercialization and Investment Readiness Microgrant
- Innovate BC demonstration programs
- PacifiCan innovation/ecosystem programs where eligible

Typical applicant requirements may include:
- for-profit B.C. company or eligible legal entity;
- IP/commercialization rights;
- matching funds;
- market/commercial outcomes;
- financial statements or operational capacity;
- customer/pilot partners.

Best Afghan Hub components:
- privacy/cybersecurity readiness;
- procurement readiness;
- commercialization strategy;
- enterprise partnership readiness;
- investment readiness;
- real-world validation where eligibility exists.

## Core rule

Do not describe the same cost as both a community-benefit expense and a commercial-development expense across funders.

Maintain:
- separate budgets;
- separate scopes of work;
- separate outcomes;
- clear IP/data ownership;
- clear contracting responsibility.

## Questions to resolve before larger applications

1. What legal entity currently owns Afghan Hub code/IP?
2. Is there a registered B.C. nonprofit associated with Afghan Hub?
3. If not, is a nonprofit partner willing to lead a specific community-benefit project?
4. Would the platform itself remain commercially owned while a nonprofit funds only a bounded community pilot?
5. Who owns data collected during a nonprofit-funded pilot?
6. Who is responsible for privacy, participant consent and reporting?
7. What costs belong to ordinary platform operations versus the funded project?
8. Can project outputs funded by a nonprofit grant remain publicly/community accessible?
9. If a commercial entity applies to Innovate BC, does it meet incorporation/good-standing and IP requirements?
10. Are related-party transactions or service-provider relationships present that must be disclosed?

## Recommended operating model to investigate

A possible model, subject to professional legal/accounting advice:

- Afghan Hub platform/IP operated by a commercial or dedicated operating entity;
- bounded community-benefit pilots delivered with an eligible nonprofit partner;
- written agreement defining scope, data handling, IP, branding, budget responsibility and reporting;
- grant funds used only for costs permitted by the specific funder;
- no assumption that one entity's eligibility transfers to another.

This is a planning concept, not legal or tax advice.

## Documents to prepare

### For nonprofit/community grants
- applicant incorporation/good-standing proof;
- organizational mandate;
- annual budget;
- project partnership agreement if Afghan Hub is a delivery partner;
- project-specific budget;
- privacy/participant plan;
- letters of support.

### For commercial/innovation grants
- incorporation/good-standing proof;
- cap table/ownership if requested;
- IP rights summary;
- financial statements;
- matching-fund evidence;
- market/customer evidence;
- service-provider quotes;
- commercialization/pilot plan.

## Decision gate before BC-ICG submission

The BC Inclusive Communities application should not be submitted until:

- eligible nonprofit applicant is confirmed;
- the applicant genuinely owns/oversees the funded community project;
- project-specific roles and budget are agreed;
- Afghan Hub's role is described accurately;
- no endorsement or partnership is implied without written consent.

## Decision gate before Innovate BC microgrant

The Commercialization and Investment Readiness Microgrant should not be pursued unless:

- an eligible B.C.-headquartered for-profit company is confirmed;
- the company has commercialization rights to Afghan Hub;
- a defined readiness barrier exists;
- an arm's-length professional provider is identified;
- 25% cash contribution is available;
- the proposed work excludes software/product development.
